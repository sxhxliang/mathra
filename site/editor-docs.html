The mathra editor

Build a formula inside a box: type it as on a calculator, or pick it from palettes, and get it back as LaTeX, MathML, SVG, PNG, speech or braille. mathra draws the box itself at every key, so what you edit is exactly what mathra will draw. Every example on this page is the editor itself, running in your browser.

Open the editor Put it in your page On npm

A tour

The whole editor, <mathra-editor>, is a few pieces put together. Each one is also a component of its own, to use alone.

Here it is, smaller than on its own page. Type, click the palettes, change the language.

Your first formula

Click the box and type. There is no need to know LaTeX: the editor reads what you type the way a calculator does. Watch the quadratic formula typed in one line, key by key:

Four things happened on the way. +- became ±; sqrt( opened a root and its ) left it; ^2 made an exponent that ended by itself at the -; and / made a fraction of the parentheses before it, dropping them, since the bar already groups. Each rule is below, with its own example.

Typing as on a calculator

Every key you would press on a calculator does what you expect. Press ▶ on any example to watch it again.

Words become what they name

Greek letters, functions, roots and large operators are typed by name: pi, theta, Delta, sin, log, sqrt, cbrt, sum, int, lim, infinity. Only the whole word counts, so point stays letters, and a word typed on becomes the longer one: sin then h is sinh.

Two keys make a symbol

<= is ≤, >= is ≥, != is ≠, -> is →, => is ⇒, +- is ±, -+ is ∓, ... is …. A space between them keeps them apart.

Fractions take what is before them

/ makes the last term the numerator: back to an operator, or a whole group in parentheses (dropped, the bar groups), or a function with its argument. The denominator ends by itself at an operator when it is a number or a name.

Exponents and indices end by themselves

^ and _ open an exponent or an index. One that is a number or a name ends at the next operator, so x^2+1 needs no braces. For more, put it in parentheses (e^(i pi)) or braces (x^{n+1}); → also leaves it.

Sums, integrals and limits

After sum, int or lim, _ and ^ go to the lower and upper limits, and a space ends them.

Prefer every key to be itself? field.editor.smartTyping = false turns all of this off; then a word is typed with \, as in LaTeX (\sqrt, \pi).

Places and the cursor

A formula is a set of nested places: a fraction has two (its numerator and its denominator), a root one or two, an exponent one, a matrix one per cell. The cursor is always in exactly one place. Here every place of a formula is outlined, each level of nesting in its own color; point at one to see what it is.

An empty place (shaded) is what a fraction, a root or a palette button leaves to fill. Tab goes to the next empty place, Shift+Tab to the one before.

Moving

← → go through every place in order, into and out of each structure; ↑ ↓ go between a numerator and its denominator, an exponent and its index, the lines of a matrix. Watch the cursor, then a letter typed where it stops:

Selecting, deleting, undoing

  • Select with Shift and the arrows, by dragging, or with a double click; Ctrl/⌘+A selects all. A palette button applied to a selection puts it inside: select a+b, press √, and it is √(a+b).
  • Backspace on a structure with something inside enters it first, so nothing is lost by accident. At the start of parentheses or a root, they go and what they held stays; in an empty denominator, the fraction goes and its numerator stays.
  • Copy and paste are LaTeX: paste $\frac{a}{b}$ from anywhere and it becomes boxes (the $ are dropped).
  • Undo (Ctrl/⌘+Z) takes back a word at a time while you type, and each palette button in one step.

Palettes and commands

Everything typed has a button, and everything with a button can be typed. Palettes come in tabs: structures, calculus, matrices, Greek, operators, relations, arrows, sets and logic, functions, accents and styles, geometry, physics, chemistry, diagrams, statistics, and ready formulas. Each button inserts its structure with empty places to fill, and is drawn by mathra from the same LaTeX it inserts.

Try the search: “integral”, “matrix”, “vector”. Or type \ and a few letters: the command menu lists what starts with them.

Commands with \

If you know LaTeX, type it: \, a name, then a space or Enter. \frac makes a fraction with two places, \alpha the letter, \pmatrix a matrix, \ce a reaction. While you type the name, a menu beside the cursor shows the commands that start with it.

Matrices

The first button of the Matrices tab opens a grid, as a table is picked in a word processor: move over it (or use the arrows) to choose the lines and columns, click or press Enter, and pick the brackets in the row under it. With the cursor in a matrix, the same grid starts at its size and changes it, marking the cells that would lose something.

Click in the matrix, then on the first button: the grid shows 2 × 2. Choose 3 × 3.

From the keyboard: & goes to the next cell, Enter to the line below (a new one at the end), Shift+Enter adds a column, Ctrl/⌘+Enter a line.

Chemistry

Reactions

Type \ce and a space (or use the Chemistry tab), then the reaction as you would write it on paper. A number after an element is its index, a number before a species its coefficient, ^ a charge, (aq) a state. ->, <=> and <-> are arrows, and [ right after one writes over it (a second [, under it).

A reaction is pieces, each in its own box: the species, the signs, and each arrow with a place over and under it. With the cursor in a reaction, a bar switches the arrow (→ ⇌ ↔ ←), adds a +, ↑ or ↓, or removes a piece; beside an arrow, its two places show as boxes to click into.

Structures

Type \chemfig and a space: the first atom is picked, ready for its label. Alt+arrow draws a bond to a new atom that way (the bar has eight directions, and a drag from an atom draws one too, snapped to 30°). Click an atom or a bond to pick it; the bar changes a bond (single, double, triple, wedge, hashed), hangs a ring, or takes any label in its box (OH, CH3, NH4^+).

Click the OH, then the bar's buttons; or pick a skeleton from the palette: its R comes picked, to type over.

Schemes

In a \schemestart, type a structure, a space, ->, its conditions in […], then the next structure. The bar picks chemfig's arrow and adds a structure or a +.

Diagrams

Commutative diagrams (tikzcd, CD, \xymatrix) are grids of cells with arrows between them. Type \tikzcd and a space, then in a cell. Alt+arrow draws an arrow from the cell that way and makes the cell it points to; its label is typed in place, and → goes on to that cell.

With the mouse: drag from one cell to another to draw an arrow, or past the cells, in any of eight directions, to make a new cell there. Click an arrow to change its style (dashed, dotted, hook, two heads, double, maps to, squiggly), bend it, move its label to the other side, or remove it.

Click an arrow to see its bar. Click in D and press Alt+→.

LaTeX blocks

Paste any formula: what the editor knows is taken apart into boxes, and what it does not (an unknown command, a units package's \qty, a structure built from submolecules) stays a block, drawn as mathra draws it and kept exactly as written. With the cursor beside a block, a bar offers Edit as LaTeX: a panel opens under it, the formula follows as you type, mathra says whether it reads it without a warning, and Ctrl/⌘+Enter finishes (Esc takes the edit back). The same panel is in every reaction, structure and diagram bar, as an escape hatch.

Click right after the quantity: its bar appears. Double click it to open the panel.

Outputs

The same formula, in every form mathra gives: LaTeX, MathML, SVG, PNG, the phrase a screen reader says (in 16 languages), braille (Nemeth, UEB, CMU and more), plain text, and the rules mathra applied with any warning. Each has copy and download.

Install

npm install @mathlabs/mathra-editor @mathlabs/mathra

@mathlabs/mathra (1.12 or later) does the drawing; the editor loads its WebAssembly by itself. With a bundler (Vite, webpack, esbuild…), nothing else is needed.

Without a bundler, map @mathlabs/mathra in an import map, as this page does:

<script type="importmap">
  { "imports": { "@mathlabs/mathra": "https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@mathlabs/mathra@1.12.0/web/index.js" } }
</script>
<script type="module" src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@mathlabs/mathra-editor/dist/index.js"></script>

In your page

Importing the package defines every element. The whole editor:

<script type="module">
  import "@mathlabs/mathra-editor";
</script>

<mathra-editor layout="page" locale="en" value="x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}"></mathra-editor>

Just a box for an answer, inside a sentence, with a few palettes:

<p>The area is <mathra-field id="answer" inline aria-label="Area"></mathra-field></p>
<mathra-toolbar for="answer" palettes="structures greek" nosearch></mathra-toolbar>

Read and set the formula as LaTeX:

const field = document.getElementById("answer");
field.value = "\\frac{1}{2}bh";                 // set it
field.addEventListener("input", (e) => {
  console.log(e.detail.latex);                 // at every change
});
field.addEventListener("change", (e) => save(e.detail.latex)); // when it loses the focus

React, Vue, Svelte

They are Web Components, so they work wherever HTML does. Listen to input for the formula.

React

import { useEffect, useRef } from "react";
import "@mathlabs/mathra-editor";

export function MathInput({ value, onChange }) {
  const ref = useRef(null);
  useEffect(() => {
    const field = ref.current;
    const listen = (e) => onChange(e.detail.latex);
    field.addEventListener("input", listen);
    return () => field.removeEventListener("input", listen);
  }, [onChange]);
  return <mathra-field ref={ref} value={value} />;
}

Vue

<!-- vite.config: vue({ template: { compilerOptions: { isCustomElement: (t) => t.startsWith("mathra-") } } }) -->
<mathra-field :value="latex" @input="latex = $event.detail.latex" />

Svelte

<mathra-field value={latex} on:input={(e) => (latex = e.detail.latex)} />

The components

Use the whole editor, or the pieces you need. A piece finds its field with for="id", or in JavaScript with piece.field = fieldElement (which also works across shadow roots).

ElementWhat it is
<mathra-editor>Everything together: the bar, palettes with their search, the field, the keyboard, the command menu and the outputs. layout="page" fills the page.
<mathra-field>The box: typing, clicking, dragging, selecting, copy and paste of LaTeX, undo. inline sizes it for a sentence.
<mathra-toolbar>Palettes in tabs, and a search over every button. palettes="structures greek" picks and orders them; nosearch hides the search; wrap shows every tab.
<mathra-palette>One grid of buttons: set="greek".
<mathra-command-menu>While \name is typed, the commands that start with it, beside the cursor.
<mathra-keyboard>A keyboard on the screen: numbers, letters, functions and symbols.
<mathra-output>LaTeX, MathML, SVG, PNG, speech, braille, text and rules, with copy and download. formats="latex mathml" picks them.
<mathra-settings>mathra's options: the font (30 to choose from), display or inline, line breaking, the braille code, strict mode, the PNG's size.
<mathra-gallery>mathra's 110 examples, drawn, with a search; choosing one opens it in the field.

The palettes are structures, calculus, matrices, greek, operators, relations, arrows, sets, functions, accents, geometry, physics, chemistry, diagrams, statistics and formulas.

Customizing

Colors and sizes

CSS variables on a component or any ancestor; they reach inside <mathra-editor> too. The defaults follow the page's color-scheme, light or dark. The formula's size follows font-size.

<style>
  mathra-field.answer {
    font-size: 28px;
    --mathra-caret: #c0392b;
    --mathra-slot: #e67e22;       /* the empty places */
    --mathra-focus: #2f7fbf;
    --mathra-radius: 4px;
  }
</style>

This field has a red cursor, blue empty places and a green focus ring.

Your own palettes

A button inserts a snippet of LaTeX, where #? is an empty place and #0 where the selection goes, or runs an action. mathra draws the button from the same snippet.

import { STRUCTURES } from "@mathlabs/mathra-editor";

toolbar.palettes = [
  STRUCTURES,
  {
    id: "physics",
    title: { en: "Physics", "pt-BR": "Física" },
    items: [
      { insert: "\\vec{F} = m\\vec{a}", title: "Newton" },
      { insert: "\\frac{d#0}{dt}", preview: "\\frac{d#?}{dt}", title: "d/dt", command: "ddt" },
      { action: "addRow", icon: "rowAdd", title: "Add a line" },
    ],
  },
];

Your own keys

field.keymap = {
  "Mod+B": "insert:mathbf",        // Mod is Ctrl, or ⌘ on a Mac
  "Mod+R": "latex:\\sqrt{#0}",
  "Mod+Enter": "",                 // turned off
};

mathra's options

import { loadFontFromCatalog } from "@mathlabs/mathra";
field.mathraOptions = { font: await loadFontFromCatalog("fira-math") };

Without a screen

@mathlabs/mathra-editor/core is the editor alone, with no DOM: for an interface of your own, a test, or a server.

import { Editor } from "@mathlabs/mathra-editor/core";

const e = new Editor();
e.typeText("x=(-b+-sqrt(b^2-4ac))/(2a)");
e.toLatex();      // "x=\\frac{-b\\pm\\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}"

e.undo();
e.insert("frac"); // a structure, with its places
e.moveDown();     // to the denominator

How it works

The editor never draws the formula itself. At every key, it writes the formula as LaTeX with a mark around each piece, mathra draws that LaTeX and says where each mark landed, and the editor puts the cursor at the edge of the right box. The marks move nothing: the drawing is exactly the one without them.

A key→the tree changes→LaTeX with marks→mathra draws→SVG and a box per mark→cursor, clicks↺

Type in this field and watch each stage follow, as the editor sees it:

1 The tree

Rows hold atoms; a structure holds rows, its places. Each has an id.

2 LaTeX with marks

Each atom and place wrapped in \htmlData{m=id}{…} (dimmed here); an operator keeps its spacing through \mathbin or \mathrel.


          
3 mathra's boxes
The SVG, and the box of every mark in its coordinates. Point at a box or a line of the tree.

        

        
A click goes to the innermost place whose box holds it; the cursor sits at the edge of the atom beside it. In a number the locale rewrites (3.14 is 3,14 in Portuguese), the number is one mark and the cursor between its digits is placed by their widths. Reactions, structures and diagrams are marked the same way, piece by piece: mathra reads through the marks inside \ce and \chemfig, and draws the chemistry as if they were not there.

        
The tests hold it to that: every one of mathra's 110 gallery examples opens in the editor and draws as it did, read, written back and marked; every palette button draws the same marked as plain.
