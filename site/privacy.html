Privacy

mathra collects nothing.

Last updated: November 12, 2026

mathra draws equations on your own device. It has no server, no account, no analytics and no cookies, and none of its packages, extensions or add-ons sends what you write to its authors or to anyone else.

This page covers every part of mathra: the libraries (Rust, npm, Python and the command line), this website, the editor, the extension for Visual Studio Code, the extension for Chrome and Edge, and the add-on for Google Docs and Google Slides. Where a part fetches something from the network, it says what, from where, and when.

What stays on your device

The equations you type, their drawings, their speech and braille, and the options you choose are computed on your device, in the browser or the program you use, by mathra's code compiled to WebAssembly or to native code. They are not sent anywhere by mathra.

Part by part

The libraries and the command line

They make no network request, with one exception you ask for: in the npm package, catalogFonts() and loadFontFromCatalog() download the catalog of math fonts and the font you name from mathfonts, hosted on GitHub Pages.

This website

  • It is hosted by GitHub Pages, which receives your requests, with your IP address, as any web host does (GitHub's privacy statement).
  • Its text fonts come from Google Fonts, which receives your IP address when your browser downloads them (Google's privacy policy).
  • The theme and the editor's language and theme you choose are kept in your browser's local storage, on your device only. There are no cookies and no analytics.
  • The playground and the editor run in your browser: what you type in them is not sent to any server. In the editor, opening the options downloads the catalog of math fonts, and choosing a font downloads that font, from mathfonts (GitHub Pages). A playground link you share carries the equation after the # of its address, a part browsers do not send to the server.

The extension for Visual Studio Code

It draws the math of your Markdown and LaTeX files inside VS Code and reads nothing outside the files you open. The settings you change are kept by VS Code. In its visual editor, opening the options downloads the catalog of math fonts, and choosing a font downloads that font, from mathfonts (GitHub Pages).

The extension for Chrome and Edge

  • contextMenus: to add "Open in mathra" to the menu of a text selection. Only when you choose it does the extension read the text you selected, to open it in mathra's editor, on your device.
  • clipboardWrite: to copy an equation (as LaTeX, MathML, SVG, an image, speech or braille) when you click Copy.

It has no access to the pages you visit otherwise, and all of its code is inside the extension. Opening the editor's options downloads the catalog of math fonts, and choosing a font downloads that font, from mathfonts (GitHub Pages).

The add-on for Google Docs and Google Slides

The add-on asks Google for these permissions, and uses them only as described:

PermissionWhat mathra does with it
See and edit the document (or the presentation) where you use the add-on, and only that onePuts the equation you insert at your cursor (or on the slide) as an image, with what a screen reader says as its alt text and its LaTeX in its link; reads the link of an equation image you select, to bring its LaTeX back to the sidebar.
Display content in a sidebarShows mathra's sidebar.
  • The add-on reads no other content of your document, and nothing of your other files.
  • The equation is drawn in the sidebar, in your browser; the image goes into your document through Google Apps Script, running in your Google account. mathra's authors have no server and receive nothing: no document, no equation, no email address.
  • The sidebar loads mathra's and its editor's code from the jsDelivr CDN, which receives your IP address (jsDelivr's privacy policy). Opening the options downloads the catalog of math fonts, and choosing a font downloads that font, from mathfonts (GitHub Pages).
  • An equation's link opens it in mathra's editor on this website, with its LaTeX after the # of the address, a part browsers do not send to the server.
  • mathra's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Your documents are also covered by Google's privacy policy.

Children

mathra is used in schools. Since it collects no personal information from anyone, it collects none from children either.

Changes

If what mathra does with data ever changes, this page will say so before the change, with a new date at the top. Its history is public in mathra's repository.
